Seattle can see one of America's most dangerous volcanoes from downtown.
Mount Rainier rises about 14,400 feet, roughly 60 miles southeast of the city, and it holds more glacier ice than any other peak in the lower 48 states. That ice is the danger: if the volcano heats up, melting snow and ice can mix with loose rock and create huge mudflows called lahars. About 5,600 years ago, the Osceola Mudflow reached the Puget Sound lowlands, where towns like Auburn stand today. Many communities now sit on old lahar deposits, so the valleys have warning sirens and marked evacuation routes. And Rainier last erupted only about a thousand years ago.
