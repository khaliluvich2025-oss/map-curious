Greenland is hiding a sheet of ice nearly two miles thick.
It covers about 80 percent of the island, an area more than twice the size of Texas. In the middle, the ice is so heavy that it has pushed the land beneath it below sea level. If all of it melted, global sea levels would rise about 24 feet. And it is melting: in recent decades, Greenland has been losing hundreds of billions of tons of ice every year, pouring water into the Atlantic. Zoom out, and the world's largest island is really a frozen reservoir, holding back a lot of ocean.
