The asteroid that wiped out the dinosaurs left a scar you can still find on the map.
About 66 million years ago, a rock roughly six miles wide slammed into a shallow sea at the northern tip of Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula. It blasted a crater about 110 miles across and helped wipe out around three quarters of all species on Earth. Today the Chicxulub crater is buried under limestone, half on land and half under the Gulf of Mexico. Oil company geologists spotted it in the late 1970s, and a curving ring of sinkholes called cenotes still traces its buried rim.
