The Everglades isn't really a swamp. It's a river. 🌾
Water spills south from Lake Okeechobee in a shallow sheet up to about 60 miles wide, creeping through sawgrass toward Florida Bay. Marjory Stoneman Douglas called it the River of Grass in 1947, the same year Everglades National Park was dedicated. Today it's about half its original size, and a huge restoration effort is trying to send that water south again.

=== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ===
The Everglades is a slow, shallow river: water flows south from Lake Okeechobee in a sheet up to about 60 miles wide toward Florida Bay. Canals, farms and cities have drained much of it, and today it is about half its original size.
Sources: National Park Service, USGS, South Florida Water Management District.
