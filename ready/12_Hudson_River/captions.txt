This river flows in two directions.
The Hudson River runs from New York's Adirondack Mountains down to New York City, but its lower half is really an estuary, an arm of the sea. Twice a day, ocean tides push in from New York Harbor and reverse the current. That tide reaches about 150 miles upstream, all the way to the Federal Dam at Troy, near Albany. In dry summers, salty ocean water can push about 60 miles up the river. Native peoples knew this long before Europeans arrived: one Native name for the Hudson is often translated as "the river that flows both ways."
