[curious] The Everglades isn't really a swamp. It's a river. [short pause] Water spills south from Lake Okeechobee and spreads into a shallow sheet up to about sixty miles wide. [confident] It creeps toward Florida Bay so slowly you can barely see it move, flowing through tall sawgrass. That's why writer Marjory Stoneman Douglas called it the River of Grass in 1947, the same year Everglades National Park was dedicated. [serious] But canals, farms and cities have drained much of it. Today the Everglades is about half its original size. [curious] Now, one of the largest restoration projects in the world is trying to send that water south again.
