[curious] This river flows in two directions. [short pause] The Hudson River runs from New York's Adirondack Mountains down to New York City. But its lower half isn't just a river. It's an estuary, an arm of the sea. [confident] Twice a day, ocean tides push in from New York Harbor and reverse the current. That tide reaches about a hundred and fifty miles upstream, all the way to the dam at Troy, near Albany. In dry summers, salty ocean water can push about sixty miles up the river. Native peoples knew this long before Europeans arrived. [curious] One Native name for the river is often translated as "the river that flows both ways."
