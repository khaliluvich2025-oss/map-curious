[curious] In 1969, engineers shut off part of Niagara Falls. [short pause] They were studying a slow problem: the falls are moving. The Niagara River carries water from Lake Erie to Lake Ontario, along the border between the United States and Canada. About twelve thousand years ago, the falls began at a cliff called the Niagara Escarpment. [confident] Since then, they've worn their way roughly seven miles upstream, carving the Niagara Gorge. The secret is in the rock. A hard cap layer sits on top of softer shale. Falling water scours the soft rock underneath until the hard ledge above breaks off. [curious] Today, erosion is much slower, partly because water is diverted for hydropower. But the falls are still creeping toward Lake Erie.
