[curious] In November 1963, fishermen off Iceland's south coast spotted dark smoke rising from the sea. [short pause] At first, they thought a ship might be on fire. It wasn't a ship. An underwater volcano was erupting. [confident] Within a day, a brand-new island broke the surface. Iceland named it Surtsey, after a fire giant from Norse mythology. The eruptions continued until 1967. By then, the island covered about one square mile. [curious] And then, something unusual happened: almost nothing. Scientists decided to leave Surtsey alone. Only researchers are allowed to land, and they work carefully not to bring seeds or insects with them. [short pause] That turned Surtsey into a natural laboratory. Scientists have watched life arrive on bare rock: seeds carried by the sea and wind, insects, and birds, which brought nutrients and more seeds. Then plants took hold. Today, Surtsey is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [serious] But the sea is winning. Waves keep wearing away the soft rock, and about half of Surtsey's original area is already gone. The island that was born in front of us is slowly disappearing.
