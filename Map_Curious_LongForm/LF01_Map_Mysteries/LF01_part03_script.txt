The United States wanted to control the shipping lanes between the Gulf of Mexico and the Atlantic, and this spot sat right beside them. Workers hauled in millions of bricks, more than sixteen million by most counts, and kept building for about thirty years. [serious] But the fort was never finished. New rifled cannons could smash brick walls, and the heavy fort was settling into the island beneath it. [short pause] During and after the Civil War, the remote fort became a prison. Its most famous inmate was Dr. Samuel Mudd, convicted after he set the broken leg of Lincoln's assassin, John Wilkes Booth. [curious] Today it's the heart of Dry Tortugas National Park, reachable only by boat or seaplane. A giant fortress, built for a war that never came to it. Now, let's head underground.
