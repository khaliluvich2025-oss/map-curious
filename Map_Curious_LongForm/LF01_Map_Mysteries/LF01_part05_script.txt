[curious] On November 24th, 1971, a man in a dark suit boarded a Northwest Orient flight from Portland, Oregon, to Seattle. [short pause] He bought his ticket under the name Dan Cooper. Once the plane was in the air, he handed a flight attendant a note. He had a bomb. [serious] In Seattle, he traded the passengers for two hundred thousand dollars and four parachutes. Then he ordered the crew to take off again and fly south, toward Mexico, low and slow. Somewhere over southwest Washington, in the dark and the rain, he lowered the rear stairs, and jumped. [short pause] He was never seen again. [confident] The FBI searched forests and rivers for years. In 1980, a boy digging along the Columbia River found about fifty-eight hundred dollars of the ransom money, still in rotting bundles. The serial numbers matched. [curious] But no body, no parachute, and no Cooper. In 2016, the FBI stopped actively investigating the case. The FBI calls it the only unsolved air piracy in American aviation history. Whether he survived that jump is still an open question. Next, we go to the bottom of the sea.
